I am a Ph.D. candidate in Political Science and a Research Associate at the [Center on the Politics of Development](https://cpd.berkeley.edu/) at the University of California, Berkeley. 

In my dissertation, I examine how individuals in rapidly evolving contexts, particularly in sub-Saharan Africa, adapt and self-organize to expand access to political rights and services. Cities around the world are expanding at unprecedented rates, widening the gap between residents' needs and the provision of basic services. I argue that neighborhoods are the primary sites where citizens organize to narrow this gap. Rather than viewing political behavior as shaped solely by the objective characteristics of one's neighborhood, I propose that how individuals perceive their neighborhoods conditions their willingness to engage in civic action to improve neighborhood welfare. I test this argument using original qualitative and quantitative data collected in Nairobi, Kenya.

Motivated by this work, I am developing open-source tools that enable scholars to more accurately capture the contexts individuals experience. I have also created [WaySurvey](https://github.com/SarahDaniel1/WaySurvey), an interactive web map that facilitates large-scale data collection, particularly in hard-to-reach settings. 

Prior to Berkeley, I earned my B.A. in International Studies from the University of California, Irvine. After graduating, I worked as a Research and Development Manager where I evaluated rural development projects in Moshi, Tanzania.
