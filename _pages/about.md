I am a Ph.D. candidate in Political Science and a Research Associate at the [Center on the Politics of Development](https://cpd.berkeley.edu/) at the University of California, Berkeley. 

My research centers on urban politics, with a particular focus on Sub-Saharan Africa. In my dissertation, I study the concept of the “neighborhood,” the least formally defined, yet most proximate political unit in everyday life. Broadly, I ask how neighborhoods function as meaningful political contexts in rapidly urbanizing settings, how their physical boundaries are subjectively defined and experienced by individuals, and how these subjective definitions shape civic engagement aimed at improving neighborhood conditions. To answer these questions, I combine both qualitative and quantitative approaches during fieldwork carried out in Nairobi, Kenya. This involves conducting in-depth interviews, leading focus group discussions, conducting mental map sessions, and administering an original in-person household survey with an embedded experiment.

Motivated by this work, I am developing open-source tools within established survey platforms that improve the measurement of local context and the design of community-based initiatives. These tools enable scholars and practitioners to more accurately capture the contexts individuals experience. Alongside these tools, I also created WaySurvey, an interactive web map that supports the implementation of large-scale data collection, particularly in hard-to-reach settings. [WaySurvey](https://github.com/SarahDaniel1/WaySurvey) enables researchers to visualize and manage study sites, thereby improving fieldwork coordination and the reliability of the data. 

Prior to Berkeley, I earned my B.A. in International Studies from the University of California, Irvine. After graduating, I worked as a Research and Development Manager where I evaluated rural development projects in Moshi, Tanzania.
