"Protest or Partnership? How Nigerian Women’s Organizations Navigate Repression"
" (with Mango Jane Angar, Leonardo R. Arriola, Matthew K. Gichohi, and Liv Tønnessen)

Abstract

Despite Nigeria’s formal protections for civic activism, women’s rights organizations rarely engage in public protest. This article examines why protest remains an uncommon tactic among these groups even in moments of democratic openness and pressing gender policy debates. Drawing on interviews with women’s rights advocates and civil society leaders, we show that protest avoidance reflects a strategic adaptation to Nigeria’s hybrid political context. Organizations confront overlapping material constraints, reputational risks, and gendered norms that render confrontation costly and often counterproductive. In response, they adopt a repertoire of quiet advocacy by framing their work in technical terms, cultivating relationships with state actors, and engaging behind the scenes to influence policy. Rather than viewing the limited visibility of women’s movements as evidence of disengagement, we argue that tactical moderation is a context-sensitive form of agency. These findings contribute to debates on civil society in authoritarian and hybrid regimes, offering a framework for understanding why activism often unfolds outside the spotlight.
